Text Boundaries and Formatting Marks in LibreOffice Writer

LibreOffice Writer provides various visual layout aids, including text boundaries and formatting marks, to help users structure documents precisely and troubleshoot spacing or alignment issues. These visual indicators appear on the screen during editing to reveal document structure, layout limits, and hidden characters, but they do not appear in the final printed document or exported PDF files.

Text Boundary Guidelines

Text boundaries provide visual frames that outline the printable content areas, margins, and embedded objects within a page. You can toggle text boundaries on or off by navigating to View > Text Boundaries in the top menu.

The primary boundary guidelines include:

  • Page Margins and Text Area: Thin, light-gray lines that outline the perimeter of the active text area, indicating where text will flow relative to page margins.
  • Header and Footer Boundaries: Dashed or solid thin lines that separate the main body text from header and footer regions.
  • Table Boundaries: Grid guidelines outlining table cells, rows, and columns even when table borders are set to none.
  • Frames and Sections: Outlines indicating the edges of inserted text frames, sections, and floating drawing objects.
  • Footnote and Endnote Areas: Separator lines and boundaries demarcating the designated space for footnotes at the bottom of pages.

To customize the color or visibility of these lines, go to Tools > Options > LibreOffice > Application Colors and adjust the settings under the Text Document section.

Formatting Mark Indicators (Non-Printing Characters)

Formatting marks reveal non-printing characters and structural breaks that dictate text flow and whitespace. You can toggle these marks on or off by pressing Ctrl + F10 or selecting View > Formatting Marks.

The following indicators can be displayed in LibreOffice Writer:

  • Paragraph Breaks (¶): Represented by a pilcrow symbol at the end of each paragraph created by pressing Enter.
  • Line Breaks (↵): Represented by a left-pointing hooked arrow, indicating a soft line break created by pressing Shift + Enter.
  • Spaces (·): Represented by a small, raised middle dot between words.
  • Non-Breaking Spaces ( ° or shaded space): Indicated by a distinct symbol or gray background shading to signify spaces created by Ctrl + Shift + Space that prevent line wrapping.
  • Tab Characters (→): Represented by a right-pointing arrow indicating a tab stop jump.
  • Soft/Optional Hyphens: Represented by a hyphen mark with a colored background or distinctive glyph, indicating a hyphen that only appears if the word breaks at the end of a line.
  • Non-Breaking Hyphens: Marked with a special gray background or highlight to show hyphens that prevent word splitting across lines.
  • Page Breaks and Column Breaks: Indicated by a colored, dashed horizontal line spanning the width of the page or column with an optional break marker icon.
  • Hidden Text: Displayed with a dotted underline beneath text formatted as hidden.
  • Bookmarks and Index Marks: Highlighted with subtle bracket markers or shaded backgrounds surrounding the designated text.

Configuring Formatting Mark Display

You can fine-tune which non-printing characters are visible without disabling all of them. Navigate to Tools > Options > LibreOffice Writer > Formatting Aids to check or uncheck individual marks, such as custom hyphens, spaces, non-breaking spaces, tabs, breaks, and hidden text.
