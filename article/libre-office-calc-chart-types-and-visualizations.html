LibreOffice Calc Chart Types and Visualizations

LibreOffice Calc offers a versatile suite of data visualization tools, ranging from traditional statistical charts to dynamic in-cell indicators. Users can transform raw spreadsheet data into visual representations using the built-in Chart Wizard, custom trend analysis tools, and conditional formatting features. This guide provides a comprehensive breakdown of every chart type and graphical visualization method supported natively in LibreOffice Calc.

Core Chart Types in LibreOffice Calc

LibreOffice Calc includes ten primary chart categories, accessible via the Insert > Chart menu. Most of these categories offer variations such as 2D, 3D, stacked, and percentage-stacked layouts.

1. Column Charts

Column charts display data using vertical rectangles. They are ideal for comparing distinct categories or tracking changes over time.

  • Variations: Normal (clustered), Stacked, and Percent Stacked.
  • 3D Views: Supports simple 3D blocks, cylinders, cones, and pyramids.

2. Bar Charts

Bar charts are the horizontal equivalent of column charts, making them useful for displaying large data labels or comparing items across categories.

  • Variations: Normal, Stacked, and Percent Stacked.
  • 3D Views: Available in box, cylinder, cone, and pyramid shapes.

3. Pie and Donut Charts

Pie and donut charts illustrate numerical proportions of a whole dataset.

  • Variations: Standard Pie, Exploded Pie (slices separated), Donut, and Exploded Donut.
  • 3D Views: Supports 3D perspective rotation, depth adjustment, and custom illumination.

4. Area Charts

Area charts emphasize the magnitude of change over time by filling the space below a line plot.

  • Variations: Normal, Stacked, and Percent Stacked.
  • 3D Views: Displays data series as layered 3D ribbons or solid blocks.

5. Line Charts

Line charts show continuous data trends over equal intervals, making them ideal for time-series analysis.

  • Variations: Points only, lines only, points and lines, and 3D lines.
  • Line Styles: Straight lines, smooth lines (cubic or B-spline curves), and stepped lines.

6. XY (Scatter) Charts

Scatter charts display the mathematical relationship between two numerical variables.

  • Variations: Points only, lines only, points and lines, and 3D lines.
  • Curve Formatting: Supports cubic splines, B-splines, and custom coordinate mapping.

7. Bubble Charts

Bubble charts extend XY scatter plots by adding a third dimension represented by the size of the bubble marker.

8. Net (Radar) Charts

Net charts plot data along radiating axes originating from a central point, useful for comparing multivariate performance metrics.

  • Variations: Points only, lines only, points and lines, and Filled Net charts.

9. Stock Charts

Designed specifically for financial analysis, stock charts track market price fluctuations.

  • Types:
    • Type 1: High, Low, Close
    • Type 2: Open, High, Low, Close
    • Type 3: Volume, High, Low, Close
    • Type 4: Volume, Open, High, Low, Close

10. Column and Line (Combination) Charts

Combination charts allow you to plot two distinct data series on the same chart using both columns and lines, with the option to use a secondary Y-axis.

  • Variations: Columns with Lines, Stacked Columns with Lines.

Analytical and Statistical Visual Overlays

Within its chart engine, LibreOffice Calc allows users to add analytical elements to enhance interpretation:

  • Trendlines: Linear, Logarithmic, Exponential, Power, Polynomial, and Moving Average lines with optional equation and R² value display.
  • Error Bars: Constant, Percentage, Standard Deviation, Standard Error, or Custom Cell Range error margins (available for X and Y axes).
  • Mean Value Lines: Automatic horizontal lines marking the statistical average of a series.
  • Gridlines and Secondary Axes: Fully customizable primary and secondary X/Y axes.

Additional Graphical Visualizations in the Grid

Beyond standalone charts, LibreOffice Calc supports in-grid graphical tools to highlight patterns directly within spreadsheet cells:

  • Sparklines: Compact, word-sized charts placed inside individual cells to show lightweight trends across rows or columns (supports Line, Column, and Stacked/Win-Loss formats).
  • Color Scales (Heat Maps): Applies gradient fills (2-color or 3-color) across a range of cells based on relative values.
  • Data Bars: Embeds horizontal progress-style bars directly inside cells to represent magnitude.
  • Icon Sets: Adds directional arrows, traffic lights, flags, or rating stars next to cell values based on specified threshold conditions.
