How TDF Ensures LibreOffice Vendor Neutrality

The Document Foundation (TDF) maintains strict vendor neutrality in LibreOffice through a structured legal framework, balanced governance rules, and a merit-based development model. By preventing any single commercial entity from dominating development, asset ownership, or executive decisions, TDF ensures LibreOffice remains an open, independent, and community-driven office suite.

Independent Legal Foundation

TDF is registered as an independent charitable foundation (Stiftung) under German law. This non-profit status requires the organization to serve the public interest rather than commercial shareholders. All core intellectual property, including the LibreOffice trademarks, domains, and foundation assets, is owned directly by TDF. Because no commercial entity owns the project's brand or infrastructure, no single company can control or alter its open-source trajectory.

Affiliation Limits on Governance

To prevent corporate takeovers of project governance, TDF enforces strict affiliation rules within its governing bodies:

  • The Board of Directors (BoD): TDF bylaws mandate that no single corporate entity or affiliated group can occupy more than one-third of the voting seats on the Board of Directors. This rule guarantees that commercial competitors must cooperate and cannot pass unilateral policies.
  • The Membership Committee (MC): The committee responsible for vetting individual voting members also enforces affiliation limits, ensuring that voting rights in foundation elections remain balanced and independent of corporate lobbying.

Meritocratic Community Membership

Governance rights in TDF are tied to individual contributors rather than corporate sponsorships. Individuals earn membership through active, sustained contributions—such as code, quality assurance, documentation, localization, or design. When voting for leadership or policy changes, members cast individual ballots based on their personal merit in the community, not on behalf of their employers.

Transparent Technical Direction

Technical decisions for LibreOffice are managed by the Engineering Steering Committee (ESC), which operates openly:

  • Open Deliberations: Technical discussions, roadmaps, and bug prioritization take place on public mailing lists, open Gerrit review systems, and publicly minuted meetings.
  • Merit-Based Code Acceptance: Code contributions from volunteer developers and paid commercial engineers (from companies like Collabora, allotropia, or Red Hat) are evaluated under the same technical standards. Merges are based on code quality and architectural fit rather than corporate influence.

A Level Playing Field for the Ecosystem

TDF actively encourages a competitive commercial ecosystem around LibreOffice without favoring specific vendors. Commercial entities are invited to provide enterprise support, custom development, and cloud solutions, but they must compete on service quality. By maintaining neutral upstream development, TDF ensures that improvements contributed by one vendor benefit the entire ecosystem and all end-users equally.
